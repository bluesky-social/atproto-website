## How sync works for spaces

TK

- Public data: each PDS emits commits, [relays](/guides/the-at-stack#relays) aggregate them into the firehose, [Jetstream](/guides/streaming-data) turns that into filterable JSON. Anyone can subscribe without permission.
- Space data isn't broadcast and has no relay. Each space is its own sync boundary.
- Records live in each member's per-space repo on their own PDS. To sync a space, fetch from every writer's PDS directly.
- Reading takes a credential: a member delegates their access, and the space authority exchanges it for a short-lived space credential.
- Notifications: a writer's PDS notifies the space host, which notifies every registered sync service. A notification only says a write happened; the service then fetches and verifies the records.
- Account changes (deactivation, deletion) are public events, so a space sync service also follows public Jetstream.
- Syncing a space doesn't mean every user of your app can see it. The app still checks who can view what.

TK: diagram (notify → fetch → one stream). See the [Spaces](/guides/spaces#syncing-a-space) walkthrough.

## Why run jetstream-spaces

TK

- Everything above, done for you: the catalogue of spaces, collecting delegations from signed-in users, exchanging and renewing credentials, registering for notifications, fetching and verifying repos, recovering from gaps, following account events.
- A Go service that runs next to your app. Your app tells it which spaces to follow and answers its requests for authorization.
- Archives what it syncs and serves it back as one Jetstream-shaped stream, with the same replay-then-live model as [Jetstream](/guides/streaming-data#jetstream).
- No Go knowledge needed: most apps run the binary and talk to it from their own language.

Route `GET /.well-known/did.json`, `POST /xrpc/com.atproto.space.notifyWrite`, and `POST /xrpc/com.atproto.space.notifySpaceDeleted` here from an HTTPS reverse proxy. TK: these route names differ from the reference PDS's `notifySpaceUpdate`; confirm.
- `--public-origin`: the HTTPS origin space hosts reach it at. Its service DID (`did:web:sync.example.com#space-sync`) is derived from this. Keep it fixed.
- `--public-jetstream-url`: public Jetstream, for account updates.
- `--data-dir`: the archive. Keep it across restarts.

### The controller

TK

- One control connection per app. Over it, jetstream-spaces asks for the **catalogue** (every space to sync) and for **authorization** (a fresh delegation from a user who can read a space).
- The controller answers authorization requests with your app's OAuth client. Your app never handles tokens; jetstream-spaces never holds OAuth sessions.

### Reading the stream

TK

- Events have the usual Jetstream commit fields plus `space`, the URI of the record's space. `did` is still the author.
- Records are always validated against your Lexicon schemas.
- TK: `live()` vs `snapshot()` vs `replay()`.

### Indexing

TK

- An indexer writing into your database is the right shape for most apps.
- Every handler is required, since records can leave a space in more ways than public records:
  - `put` / `del` per record type
  - `deleted`: the space was deleted
  - `authorRemoved`: an author left the space
  - `authorReset`: an author's records are being re-imported
  - `account`: deactivated (hide) or deleted (purge), in every space
- Key tables by space as well as author, collection, and rkey.
- At-least-once delivery: make handlers idempotent.

## What your app still handles

TK

- Viewer access: the stream holds every followed space. Backend only, never browsers.
- Blobs: only blob refs are synced. Fetch blobs with the viewer's own authorization.
