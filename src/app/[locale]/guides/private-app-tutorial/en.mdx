TK intro.

- A private calendar: events stored in a personal space, non-public data that only you (and apps you authorize) can read.
- Personal spaces have one member, and the user is the space authority. Good fit for settings, drafts, bookmarks, notes.
- Shout out [Airspace](https://getair.space/), which keeps drafts in a personal space until you publish them.
- Same shape as the [bot tutorial](/guides/bot-tutorial): password login and a small script, plus a page you can open in your browser.
- Uses the published [Lexicon Community](https://lexicon.community/) calendar Lexicons, so there's nothing to define or publish.

## Prerequisites

TK

- Node.js 22+ (TK: confirm minimum for `@atproto/lex-space`).
- An account on a Spaces-enabled PDS. During the alpha, get an invite from your [BPS account](https://bsky.network/account), or [run your own](/blog/atproto-spaces-alpha#running-a-spaces-capable-pds).

<Note>
App passwords can't access spaces. This tutorial logs in with your full account password, so use a test account. Production apps should use [OAuth](/guides/auth).
</Note>

## Part 1: Lexicons

TK

- `community.lexicon.calendar.collection`: a published space type for calendar data, holding events and RSVPs.
- `community.lexicon.calendar.event`: the event record.
- Install and build them like any other published Lexicon. TK: verify `lex install` handles space types in the alpha.

<S03 />

## Part 2: Log in

TK

- Save shared code in `src/calendar.ts`.
- Same as the bot tutorial, but with your full account password, and returning a `Client`.

<S04 />

## Part 3: Open your calendar

TK

- The calendar space type uses `"key": "any"`, so pick a space key: `personal`.
- Look for it with `listSpaces`; create it the first time.
- `personalspace` policy: only the authority has access.
- Everything here goes to your own repo on your own PDS, so no space credential is needed.

<S05 />

## Part 4: Add and remove events

TK

- CLI in `src/index.ts`.
- `create`, `delete`, `list` mirror the public record helpers from [Writing public data](/guides/writing-data).
- Prints your calendar after every command.

## Part 5: View it in your browser

TK

- `src/server.ts`: a tiny `node:http` server that reads your calendar and renders it as HTML.
- Only listens on `127.0.0.1`: the page shows private data, and there's no login.

TK
