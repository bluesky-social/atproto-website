_Jay Graber helped lead the development of atproto as the CEO of Bluesky, and is now the Chief Innovation Officer (CIO) at Bluesky. In this essay, she shares her perspective on AI and the choices facing people building on the network._

I want people to have more say in the software they use, and I want developers to be able to build useful things without locking people in. A new app on atproto can work with existing identities and compatible public records. A developer can spend more time on the experience they want to create, with less to build before someone can try it.

Consider a cinema app. It could let someone sign in with their existing account, find people they already follow, and start sharing film reviews. The developer would need to support those connections and define records for reviews, but people could arrive with something familiar to work with. The app would still need to earn their interest.

AI coding tools make it faster to try an idea like this. Existing identity and data make that faster iteration more useful: a developer can test an experience with real records sooner. That could help someone with limited time or programming experience build a useful first version.

The same questions about control apply to the tools themselves. Who chooses the model? What data can it access? Can someone change providers or stop using it? The values behind atproto give builders a useful starting point.

## Our values

The principles that guide our AI work are the same ones that guide everything else we build.

**User choice and user control.** You should decide what you see, who you trust, and which tools act on your behalf. No single company should make those decisions for you.

**Empowering people to build their own spaces online.** The web is better when people can shape their own corner of it, not just build on someone else's platform.

**Diversifying the web.** A healthy internet has many apps, many communities, and many different approaches, all able to interoperate.

**Building a healthy information ecosystem.** People need to be able to tell what's real, where it came from, and who is behind it.

## Putting our values into practice

### AI is optional

You don’t need AI to use atproto. Some apps will put AI at the center of the experience; others won’t use it at all. People should be free to choose the apps and experiences that work for them.

### Bring your own model

We support open weight models. People and developers can inspect them, run them on their own infrastructure, and swap them out. Whatever AI features we build, you should be able to plug in your own model instead of being locked into ours.

### AI for building, not for noise

AI is most useful on an open network when it lowers the barrier to making something of your own: a custom feed, a community space, a tool that fits how you work. We aren't interested in making it easier to fill timelines with generated content.

### AI for finding what matters

As the volume of content grows, people need better feeds, filters, search, and moderation. AI can power those tools, and people should be the ones who choose and configure them.

## Agents acting on someone’s behalf

An agent that schedules a meeting or posts for someone needs permission to do that work. The person using it needs to understand that permission and be able to withdraw it. Other people may also need to know who the agent represents. For example, someone might let an agent draft a reply while requiring approval before it sends anything. The tools people use to set and review permissions should make that distinction clear.

## Building agents on atproto

The AT Protocol wasn't designed specifically for AI, but the properties that make it good for people also make it a strong foundation for agents.

**A common identity layer.** Every account on atproto has a portable, cryptographic identity (a DID) that works across every app on the network. An agent can have its own identity in the same system people use, so it can be recognized, referenced, and held to the same norms, whichever app you encounter it in.

**Signed records.** Atproto repositories contain signed records. Those signatures can help establish which account published a record. They do not prove that an agent’s account of its work is accurate, or capture actions it takes outside the network. Linking an agent to the person responsible for it requires an additional, explicit relationship.

**Infrastructure you can self-host.** Your Personal Data Server (PDS) holds your data, and you can run it yourself or choose who runs it for you. That makes the PDS a natural personal context layer: a place where your preferences, history, and permissions live under your control, which agents you invite can draw on, and which you can revoke.

## Building it together

We don't have every answer about AI, and no single company should. What we can do is build open infrastructure where these questions get worked out in public, by many people, with people in control.

If you're building agents, AI tools, or moderation services, we'd love for you to build them on atproto, and to help shape the norms for how agents identify themselves and behave on an open network. We’ll be sharing more soon, so follow along and tell us what you think!
